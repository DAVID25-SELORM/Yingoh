### B01 · Part B · Physiological Adaptation

*Cardiovascular — Heart failure · Analyze Cues · medium · single answer*

A nurse reviews data for a client admitted with heart failure. Weight: 70.2 kg on day 1, 71.4 kg on day 2, and 72.9 kg on day 3. The client has new bibasilar crackles, 2+ pitting ankle edema, urine output of 600 mL over 24 hours, BP 148/92 mmHg, and SpO2 93% on room air. Which analysis of these cues is most accurate?

- A. The weight gain reflects expected improvement in nutrition.
- B. The client has developed a bacterial pneumonia.
- C. The client is dehydrated from excessive diuretic therapy.
- D. The client is developing fluid volume overload from worsening heart failure.  **✓ correct**

**Rationale:** Weight rose about 2.7 kg in 48 hours (1 kg is roughly 1 L of retained fluid) along with new crackles, edema, hypertension and low urine output. Together these cues show fluid retention from worsening heart failure. Dehydration would cause weight loss, and nothing in the data points to infection.

**Why each option:**
- A: A gain of 2.7 kg in 48 hours with new crackles and edema is retained fluid, not improved nutrition.
- B: Pneumonia usually causes fever, a productive cough and focal findings, and would not explain the edema and weight gain.
- C: Dehydration causes weight loss, dry mucous membranes and concentrated urine, not weight gain with crackles and edema.
- D: Rapid weight gain with new crackles, edema and low urine output shows retained fluid from worsening heart failure.

### B02 · Part B · Physiological Adaptation

*Medical-Surgical — Diabetic ketoacidosis · Analyze Cues · medium · single answer*

A client with type 1 diabetes is admitted with a glucose of 486 mg/dL, deep rapid respirations, and a fruity breath odor. Arterial blood gases show pH 7.21, PaCO2 26 mmHg, and HCO3 12 mEq/L. Which interpretation is correct?

- A. Metabolic acidosis with respiratory compensation  **✓ correct**
- B. Metabolic alkalosis with respiratory compensation
- C. Respiratory alkalosis without compensation

**Why each option:**
- A: A low pH with a low HCO3 shows metabolic acidosis, and the low PaCO2 shows respiratory compensation.
- B: Metabolic alkalosis would show a high pH and a high bicarbonate.
- C: Respiratory alkalosis would have a high pH; here the pH is low and the low PaCO2 is compensation.
- D: Respiratory acidosis would show a high PaCO2, not a low one.

*Laboratory and Diagnostics — Hyperkalemia · Analyze Cues · medium · single answer*

### D10 · Part D · Physiological Adaptation

*Emergency and Critical Care — Tension pneumothorax · Prioritize Hypotheses · hard · single answer*

A client receiving mechanical ventilation suddenly has hypotension, absent breath sounds on the right, tracheal deviation to the left, and distended neck veins. What should the nurse do first?

- A. Administer a PRN sedative
- B. Increase the ventilator rate
- C. Notify the provider immediately and prepare for emergency needle decompression while maintaining oxygenation  **✓ correct**
- D. Obtain a portable chest x-ray and wait for the results before acting

**Rationale:** These findings indicate a tension pneumothorax, which compresses the heart and lungs and can cause cardiac arrest within minutes. It is treated clinically with immediate decompression, not after imaging.

**Why each option:**
- A: Sedation would worsen instability and does not treat the cause.
- B: Increasing the ventilator rate raises chest pressure and can worsen the tension.
- C: These signs indicate a tension pneumothorax that needs immediate decompression.
- D: Waiting for imaging delays lifesaving decompression.
